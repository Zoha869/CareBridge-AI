MEDICATION INFORMATION — GENERAL EDUCATIONAL CONTENT

This section provides general information about common medication
categories. It does not state personalized doses and is not a
substitute for a doctor's or pharmacist's instructions. A patient's
actual prescription and doctor instructions are always the primary
source for personalized medication questions. This system does not
independently prescribe, discontinue, or change medication.

---

PARACETAMOL / ACETAMINOPHEN

Common Uses: Relief of mild to moderate pain and reduction of fever.

General Administration Information: Usually taken by mouth, with or
without food, following the dose and frequency on the label or as
prescribed by a doctor.

Common Side Effects: Generally well tolerated at recommended doses;
rare side effects can include allergic reactions or nausea.

Important Precautions: Exceeding the recommended dose can cause serious
liver damage. Patients should check other medications for overlapping
paracetamol/acetaminophen content before combining them, and should ask
a doctor before use if they have liver disease or drink alcohol
regularly.

---

IBUPROFEN (NSAID)

Common Uses: Relief of pain, inflammation, and fever.

General Administration Information: Usually taken with food or milk to
reduce stomach upset.

Common Side Effects: Stomach upset, heartburn; less commonly, stomach
ulcers or bleeding with prolonged use.

Important Precautions: Should be used cautiously or avoided by people
with a history of stomach ulcers, kidney disease, certain heart
conditions, or during late pregnancy — a doctor should be consulted in
these situations. May interact with blood thinners and some blood
pressure medications.

---

AMOXICILLIN (ANTIBIOTIC)

Common Uses: Treatment of certain bacterial infections, as prescribed
by a doctor.

General Administration Information: Taken by mouth at evenly spaced
intervals; the full prescribed course should be completed even if
symptoms improve early, unless a doctor advises otherwise.

Common Side Effects: Nausea, diarrhea, or rash.

Important Precautions: Should not be used by patients with a known
penicillin allergy. Antibiotics do not treat viral infections such as
the common cold or flu.

---

METFORMIN

Common Uses: Commonly used to help manage blood sugar levels in type 2
diabetes.

General Administration Information: Usually taken with meals to reduce
stomach upset, as directed by a doctor.

Common Side Effects: Stomach upset, diarrhea, or a metallic taste,
especially when starting treatment.

Important Precautions: Should be used cautiously in people with kidney
problems. Patients should inform their doctor before certain imaging
procedures that use contrast dye, as it may need to be paused
temporarily.

---

AMLODIPINE (BLOOD PRESSURE MEDICATION)

Common Uses: Used to treat high blood pressure and certain types of
chest pain (angina).

General Administration Information: Usually taken once daily, at
roughly the same time each day.

Common Side Effects: Swelling of the ankles or feet, flushing,
dizziness, or headache, particularly when starting treatment.

Important Precautions: Should not be stopped suddenly without a
doctor's guidance, as this can affect blood pressure control.

---

OMEPRAZOLE (ACID REDUCER)

Common Uses: Used to reduce stomach acid for conditions such as acid
reflux or stomach ulcers.

General Administration Information: Usually taken before a meal, as
directed.

Common Side Effects: Headache, stomach pain, or nausea.

Important Precautions: Long-term use should be reviewed periodically
with a doctor, as prolonged acid suppression has been associated with
certain nutrient absorption changes.

---

CETIRIZINE / LORATADINE (ANTIHISTAMINES)

Common Uses: Relief of allergy symptoms such as sneezing, itching, and
runny nose.

General Administration Information: Usually taken once daily.

Common Side Effects: Mild drowsiness (more common with cetirizine),
dry mouth.

Important Precautions: Caution advised when driving or operating
machinery until the individual's response to the medication is known.

---

GENERAL MEDICATION SAFETY NOTES

- Always follow the dose, frequency, and duration given by the
  prescribing doctor or on the medication label, not general
  information such as this.
- Do not stop, change, or combine medications without checking with a
  doctor or pharmacist first, especially for chronic conditions.
- Report any known allergies before starting a new medication.
- Store medications as directed on the label, generally in a cool, dry
  place away from children's reach unless otherwise specified.
- If a dose is missed, follow the specific instructions provided with
  that medication rather than doubling the next dose without guidance.
